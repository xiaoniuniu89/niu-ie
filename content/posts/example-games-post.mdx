I've been playing board games seriously for about a decade now, and there are some absolute gems that never seem to get the attention they deserve. Here are three that I think belong in every collection.

## Arboretum

A deceptively simple card game about planting trees. You're building paths of ascending values in different tree species, but the scoring mechanism is brutal — you only score a species if you hold the most total value of that species in your hand at the end. Every card you keep for scoring is a card you can't play to the table.

It's one of the most agonising games I've ever played, in the best way.

## Concordia

On the surface, Concordia looks dry — Roman merchants trading goods around the Mediterranean. But the card-play mechanism is elegant. Every action you take is a card you play from your hand, and those same cards determine your final score. There are no points during the game. You just play, and at the end, everything clicks into place.

## Patchwork

A two-player game about quilting. Yes, quilting. And it's genuinely tense. You're fitting Tetris-like pieces onto your board, managing a button economy, and racing along a time track. It takes ten minutes to teach and a lifetime to master.
